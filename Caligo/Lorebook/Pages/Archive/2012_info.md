# All About The Equus Avis
#### Anatomy
They live around normal horse years, maturing at three and living to a maximum of 30 years. They range in height from 12hh to 15hh, a huge range.  
The coats of these beasts consist of hair and fine feathers, which are incredibly soft, but fragile and easily extracted when the animal is dead, meaning that skins from EQAs are not so sought after. As with normal horses, the coat grows thick in the winter, and in some cases, more vibrant colours become paler, and in summer the coat is thinner.  
Their features include large, birdlike eyes, very small nostrils, and small ears.  

The bones of these animals are similar to [bird's bones](https://svpow.com/wp-content/uploads/2011/11/sanded-turkey-vertebra-full.jpg), in that they are very light and fragile, with pockets of air inside. This means that they cannot take much weight, and so it is difficult for them to be ridden without causing serious damage.

#### Colouration and Patterning
Most colours occur, ranging from base bays blacks and browns to roan and dun. Most have bird catcher and/ or bend or spots, and they sometimes occur on the body in tones of their feather colour. But this is quite rare. Appaloosa markings are not so likely to occur, but are possible due to a small amount of Knabstrupper blood in this species' stream.

#### Characterisation
A small amount of characterisation is allowed, e.g changing the length of the mane, making it look thicker or wispier etc. Small accessories are also allowed, like headcollars, or braids. You may not overload them with accessories, one or two maximum.

#### Habitat
They live in northern and mid- northern Europe, with the largest populations in countries such as Poland, Lithuania, Latvia and Belarus. Some colonies have been found to survive as far north as finland and mid Russia. Some live in southern Sweden and Denmark (Denmark also is the area where most spotted Avis' are found), but are not so common in Norway.
They usually live in areas of high ground, hilly and mountainous parts are usual. It is not unheard of to find herds near the sea, by areas with large cliffs or drops. These animals are able to live on flat plains land, too, tending to stay in larger herds in these areas.
