Intro


This journal hosts information pertaining to the Equus Avis’ eating, breeding, and behavioural habits, their vocalisations, history, traditions and habitat.


Appearance

Fine, delicate features. Female-exclusive fangs (males may very rarely have fangs, but they will never be larger than half the length of a female fang). The muscles in their faces can contract and relax to bring their fangs tilted forward or backwards.
Manes are often unkempt and unruly, at varying lengths depending on the climate in which they live.
Have thick, fluffy ears to help wind reduction and prevent parasites.
Thin haired, water resistant coat.
Heights have extreme variation, ranging from 9hh to 15hh, with the lower and upper bounds occurring in only particular geographical locations naturally, or artificially due to breeding. Equus Aves smaller than 11hh are rare, though they are known to exist. Most imports will be between 11.3hh and 14.3hh.


Conformation

Short pasterns - The majority of this breed have exceptionally short pasterns, which while creating a somewhat bone jarring gait to attempt sitting with, help with fast take offs.
Little to no crest - Both mares and stallions possess little to no crest, as the added weight would merely be a hinderance to their flying abilities and cause unnecessary stress to their joints.
Herron gutted - While not an aesthetically pleasing looking quality, this particular fault helps to eliminate wind resistance while flying, as well as make it near to impossible to keep a saddle on.

Diet

Omnivorous, with the diet consisting mostly of veggies, but sometimes the odd insect, small bird, or mammal, and in exceptional cases, fish. Lichen, moss, and bark is often consumed. Rarely do they catch and eat anything larger than a pheasant. Mammals larger than a badger tend to put up a pretty vicious fight.


Communication

These creatures communicate with a wide variety of vocalisations. Those that resemble their equine cousins': the horse's knicker, and variations on zebra and donkeys' bays, but also screeches, chirps, and in some cases, song-like whistles of birds.
In interaction and greeting, more equine vocalisations are heard, whereas in warning, mating, and terror, bird vocalisations are more common.
Both mares and stallions may perfect 'mating song' as a part of the mate-finding process, and perform it during mating season. It resembles bird song.


Behaviour

When herding, they tend to stick to smaller groups than larger ones. They live in a matriarchal society, though the majority of loners are non-mating females.
Location affects the behaviours of individual and herded Equus Avis. In mountainous regions, herds tend not to exceed fifteen members. Here they live in caves and shelter beneath rock overhangs. In wooded areas, Equus Avis may nest, on the ground or atop trees. Baths are taken in dirt.

Location

Although originally found in several mountainous parts of South Africa, these parasites have managed to spread and adapt to multiple places all around the world, and currently reside in most of the northern hemisphere and some areas between the equator and the tropic of Capricorn (though those that live here tend to be on the smaller side (less than 11hh) and have less dense feathering and hairs).



Traditions

It is traditional for an Equus Avis to be named respective to the country it was imported from, for example, an EQA imported from Iceland, may be given a traditional Icelandic horse's name. In most cases, the country of origin will be up to you, and of course, this tradition is not compulsory.
