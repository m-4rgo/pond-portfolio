# Tack
There are 2 tpyes of saddles in the given lore; the chairseat saddle and the race saddle.

_* Note: these saddles were designed for Penequus gracilis; the Pelequus' batwings necessitate a slightly different tack configuration involving a crupper._

![saddle](https://www.chickensmoothie.com/oekaki/image/image.php?id=4372566&size=large&format=auto&rev=1768224342)
`Fig. 6: Chairseat Saddle`  
The chairseat positions the rider's legs over and in front of the wings, ensuring enough clearance as not to hinder their movement. The chairseat is a comfortable seat for long distance or leisure riding, but due to the legs' position over the shoulder (slightly impeding the movement of the shoulder and forelegs), it is not the most ideal seat for ground racing. The chairseat is considered the most ancient riding style for pterohippi- early riding tack was uncovered as a series of ropes tied around the chest of the horse, providing loops that acted as stirrups in the same position as the modern chairseat saddle.

![saddle](https://www.chickensmoothie.com/oekaki/image/image.php?id=4372399&size=large&format=auto&rev=1768160959)
`Fig. 7: Race Saddle`  
The race saddle requires the rider to assume a kneeling position, with their knees resting on the front knee-rolls and toes planted in the rear stirrups. It's a physically demanding position, requiring the rider to shift weight between the knees and feet as the ride demands. The advantage of this seat is that the rider's legs are kept well clear of the mount's shoulder and forelegs, meaning that takeoffs and ground galloping can be much faster, with the added benefit of the rider's more aerodynamic position. The race position was developed much later than the chairseat, as it's near impossible to assume the race position on the back of a pterohippus without the aid of tack.
