# Intro


Tuskur is just the type of accessory that EQAs wear! It's often seen as a status symbol.
This journal lists the standard for base tuskur and influenced tuskur, look here for guidance when designing tuskur for your Equus Avis!
We do not mind if you use influenced tuskur in your external characters, but humbly request that you keep base tuskur within your Equus Aves.


## What tuskur pass do I need?


You need to buy a tuskur pass for every region your tuskur passes through. If it passes  over a fuzzy area/ border to zones, you only have to buy one zone. If tuskur is drawn onto your EQA's official reference before you adopt it, you do not need to purchase any tuskur passes for that tuskur.


## Base Tuskur

Base tuskur is characterised by lightweight filigree jewellery, and translucent gossamer fabrics (drapes).
Tuskur tends to often see multi-coloured fabrics and can incorporate precious stones into designs too. Loudly patterned tuskur is rare; the quieter pattern is uncommon but still used. Small tassels appear, as well as larger "cutrain pull" type tassels. Piercing is not uncommon, as it is often used as an "anchor" to secure drapes in place.
Filigree metal is essential to the style. Filigree can appear without drapes, but drapes may never appear unsecured by filigree. Filigree is only seen as gold, alloys with gold, or red and yellow metals such as copper and brass. Green is permitted on copper filigree. Silver is never used (except in white gold, where the silver content may not exceed 50%, causing a yellow hue to be retained). The same applies for other silver metals like platinum and nickel. Metals will NOT be mixed on an individual; filigree will be only one type of metal or alloy on one EQA at any one time. Aged gold is permitted. The diagram below may help with colour choice.
![diagram](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e7/Ag-Au-Cu-colours-english.svg/350px-Ag-Au-Cu-colours-english.svg.png)

Equus avis headgear is unique in combining fabrics with metal pieces and chains, for lightweight but attractive headcollars and bridles. Fabrics act as connective components, that help keep the headgear in place.
Piercings and bracelets are not considered Equus Avis exclusive, (although are essential to the style) and you may add these as stand-alone jewellery to your EQA as you wish. A tuskur pass is required when you wish to add drapes.
Hairstyle is not dictated by tuskur; you may use any combination of plaits, cuts, and styles without requirement for a tuskur pass.

## Influenced Tuskur

Because of the scattered nature of EQAs habitat, tuskur can take influence from a wide range of cultures' adornments, making it a very customisable field. Although we differentiate this from base tuskur, a tuskur pass is still required for its use. The ONLY exception is the use of modern day headcollars and show bridles. We do not reccomend the use of modern day saddles with equus aves, as it does not correlate well with EQAs' anatomy. There is a free EQA specific tack set available here, whose colours you may customise at will.

What we consider to be influenced tuskur is tuskur which combines aspects of base tuskur with external styles. For example, the following:
Equus Avis, ID#00123 by star-gutz
[This piece](https://www.deviantart.com/heystardust/art/Equus-Avis-ID-00123-544116563) combines drapes with a stylised bitless bridle.
This piece uses piercings and a drape, but also incorporates a fabric collar, which is seen with akhal-teke horses.
ALL metals and alloys are permitted in influenced tuskur.
If you are referencing completely a pre-existing stlye (for example, using a leather lusitano bridle) or combining two external styles (combining western and arabian, for example) you do not need a tuskur pass! Please ensure your tack does not emulate base tuskur in this instance.
